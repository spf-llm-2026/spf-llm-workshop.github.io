About the Workshop

Why SPF-LLM?

Large language models are increasingly deployed in high-stakes settings — healthcare, law, finance, national security — yet fundamental questions about their security and privacy properties remain underexplored. Attacks can manipulate outputs through jailbreaks, prompt injection, and adversarial inputs; training pipelines can be poisoned; private data can be extracted through memorization and inference attacks.

SPF-LLM creates a dedicated venue for work that rigorously examines these failure modes and proposes theoretically grounded, empirically validated defenses. We welcome work spanning the full LLM lifecycle — from pretraining and fine-tuning to inference, deployment, and monitoring.

🔐

Jailbreaks & Adversarial Attacks

Semantic, syntactic, and mathematical encoding attacks that bypass safety alignment; detection and defense methods.

🛡️

Privacy & Memorization

Membership inference, training data extraction, differential privacy in LLMs, and federated learning.

☠️

Supply Chain & Poisoning

Data poisoning, model tampering, backdoor attacks, and AI supply chain integrity.

🤖

Agentic & Multi-Agent Security

Safety risks in agentic pipelines, tool-use hijacking, orchestration vulnerabilities, and multi-agent trust.

⚖️

Evaluation & Benchmarking

Benchmarks for safety, red-teaming methodologies, automated evaluation, and LLM-as-judge reliability.

📋

Regulation & Governance

Policy frameworks, compliance mechanisms, auditing standards, and responsible disclosure for LLMs.

Submissions

Call for Papers

We invite submissions on all aspects of security and privacy for large language models. We especially encourage work that bridges theory and practice, introduces new benchmarks or datasets, or proposes novel evaluation frameworks.

Topics of Interest

Submissions may address (but are not limited to) the following areas:

Jailbreak attacks and detection methods
Prompt injection and indirect prompt injection
Adversarial robustness of LLMs
Privacy attacks: membership inference, data extraction
Differential privacy and federated LLM training
Data and model poisoning / backdoor attacks
AI supply chain integrity
Agentic and multi-agent security
Red-teaming methodologies and tools
LLM evaluation and safety benchmarking
Watermarking and provenance of LLM outputs
Governance, auditing, and regulatory compliance

Submission Formats

Regular Papers Up to 8 pages (excl. references)
Short Papers Up to 4 pages (excl. references)
Position Papers Up to 4 pages (excl. references)

All submissions must follow the NeurIPS 2026 format. Submissions are double-blind. Previously published work and work under concurrent review are not eligible. Accepted papers will be posted on the workshop website (non-archival).
